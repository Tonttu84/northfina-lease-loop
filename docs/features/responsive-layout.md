# Responsive layout: every page at every screen size, language and text size

## What it does

`e2e/10-responsive-layout.spec.ts` opens 19 pages of the built app, each as the actor who sees it and after the scenario card it needs, in the four shipped languages and two generated pseudo-languages, at nine screen sizes from a 320 px phone to a 2560 px screen and at the reader's 150 % and 200 % text, and asks the browser itself what is broken: sideways scroll, something out of the viewport (also during an animation), text escaping or clipped, two controls or texts drawn over each other, a word broken that fits its line, a tap target under 24 px on a phone, text that ignores the reader's size; where the header's links fold into its menu, it checks the page again with the menu open. It answers the question "is the frontend truly responsive" with a list of elements and numbers instead of screenshots someone has to look at; it passes, with no finding (the 889 findings of its first full runs and the 1 436 of the checks the PR 27 review added were fixed in the layout, never in the checks), and two more tests pin the design rules issue 19 asked for: the header keeps one row when someone is signed in (D99), and wide screens get wider lists while forms and the contract stay narrow (D98).

## The rule

**The checks (`e2e/layout.ts`, `checkLayout` and `checkTextScales`).** Each runs in the page on the layout as it is now, on every rendered element (`checkVisibility()`: the content of a closed `<details>` such as the header's menu or the price breakdown is laid out but not shown, and a screen-reader-only 1 px box is off the page on purpose; both were false positives before they were excluded).

| Check                              | Fails when                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Why it exists                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pageScroll`                       | the document is wider than the viewport; the detail names the box that reaches furthest, a hidden one included (a `visibility: hidden` tooltip still widens the page)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | WCAG 2.2 1.4.10 (reflow): content at 320 CSS px needs no scrolling in two directions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `outOfViewport`                    | an element reaches past either edge and no ancestor below `<body>` scrolls sideways (`overflow-x: auto`, `scroll`: a table's wrapper holds its table; `hidden` and `clip` cut the content off where nobody can scroll to it, so they hold nothing); only the outermost offender is listed                                                                                                             | names the element that causes `pageScroll`, and catches what sticks out of a page that clips it.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `animatedOverflow`                 | the same, at any of seven points (0 % to 100 %) of a running CSS animation, sampled with the animation paused and put back                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | the settled page can look fine while an entrance animation pokes past the edge: the item page's life stamp starts `stamp-press` at `scale(1.18)` and wobbles a phone sideways for 420 ms on every load.                                                                                                       |
| `textOverflow`                     | text is wider than its own box (`scrollWidth > clientWidth`) in an element that lets it spill (`overflow-x: visible`); for an inline element (a `<span>`, a link, which has no box of its own), a line of it reaches past the block it sits in                                                                                                                                                        | a word or number that cannot wrap (a Finnish compound, a date, an e-mail address) runs over its neighbour even when nothing reaches the viewport's edge.                                                                                                                                                      |
| `overlap`                          | two controls or texts in the page's flow cross each other by more than 2 px each way; a layer of its own (absolute, fixed, sticky: the menu panel, a tooltip, a stamp on a drawing), a transformed box (its rectangle is the bounding box of the rotated shape) and a box inside the other do not count; an inline text counts line by line, one line-height tall each                                | the PR 27 review found the staff strip's `<select>` squeezed to its arrow and drawn over the "Demo" link at 390 px with 200 % text: every box inside the viewport, no text spilling, and still broken.                                                                                                        |
| `brokenWord`                       | a word of a language (letters only, with the punctuation glued to it; no e-mail address, number or id, no `break-all` text) is broken across lines although it is at least 1 px narrower than its column: the nearest box whose width does not come from its content (not inline, not a table cell, not a flex item, not absolutely positioned), less the padding and borders of the boxes in between | `overflow-wrap: break-word` turns an overflow into a silent break mid-word. A word longer than its column has to break and is not listed; a word that fits means a box gave way in the wrong order (a label squeezed before its value moved under it) or the browser hyphenated a word to even out the lines. |
| `textOverlapsBelow`, `textClipped` | text is taller than its box by more than half a line: running over what follows (`overflow-y: visible`) or cut off                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | a fixed height the text outgrew. Up to half a line is the glyphs' ink above and below a tight line height, which every large heading has (run 2 flagged 468 such headings by 4 to 7 px; nobody sees it).                                                                                                      |
| `smallTargets`                     | on a phone (430 px or narrower), a link, button, field or `summary` whose tap area is under 24 × 24 CSS px, except a link inside a sentence and an undersized target with room around it (a 24 px circle on its centre touches no other target and no other undersized target's circle)                                                                                                               | WCAG 2.2 2.5.8 (target size, minimum) with its exceptions. The tap area is the larger of the control and what taps it: its own `<label>` (the 13 px radios sit in 44 px labels) and a link stretched over its card by an absolute `::after`.                                                                  |
| `textNotScaling`                   | with the root font size doubled in place, an element's text grows less than 1.5 times                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | WCAG 2.2 1.4.4 (resize text): a size in `px` ignores the reader's setting. Run once per page and language.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

**Metrics, and the two design rules asserted on them.** `checkLayout` also returns the widest element inside `<main>` that is not full-bleed (what the content uses), the most columns any grid shows and the header's height (the staff strip's and the header's); the spec attaches them to the report as `layout-metrics`. How much of a wide screen the content uses is a design decision, not breakage, so the page checks do not judge it; two tests at the end of the spec assert the decisions taken:

| Test                                                       | Asserts                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `the header keeps one row when someone is signed in`       | On `/catalogue` at 768 × 1024, 1024 × 1080 and 1100 × 1080 in en, fi, sv and et, the header's height as `cust-aino` and as `admin` equals its height signed out (issue 19, D99). Equal rather than a bound in pixels: whatever the header's one row measures, signing in adds nothing to it. 768 px is the tablet that wrapped, 1024 px where the links come out of the menu, 1100 px a width between that and the 1280 px laptop. Written first and seen to fail in run 6: 170 px signed in against 126 px signed out at 768 px in all four languages (the nav wrapped onto a second row). |
| `wide screens get wider lists and keep narrow forms (D98)` | The catalogue's grid shows 3 columns at 1440 px and 4 at 1920 and 2560 px; at 2560 px the catalogue and my leases use 1280 px, the sign-in form 672 px and card 1's contract 1024 px; at 1440 px the catalogue still uses 1152 px (below 1536 px nothing changes). Written first and seen to fail in run 6: 3 columns at 1920 and 2560 px, the catalogue 1152 px and my leases 896 px at 2560 px; the contract's line seen to fail in run 10 (1280 px) before the owner's exception was built.                                                                                              |

**The menu open.** Below 1024 px (and at 1280 px with 200 % text, where `lg` is 2 048 px) the header's links are only in its menu (D99), so at every viewport where the menu button shows, the page is checked a second time with `details[data-nav-menu]` open (findings marked `menu open`). Run 10 found the panel at −18 to 366 px at 390 px with 150 % text and −154 to 358 px with 200 %.

**The viewports.** 320 × 640, 360 × 780 and 390 × 844 (phones), 844 × 390 (a phone in landscape), 768 × 1024 (tablet), 1280 × 800, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440; then 150 % and 200 % text on 390, 768 and 1280: fifteen in all.

**The reader's text size is the browser's setting.** `useViewport` waits for `document.fonts.ready` and two frames after it sets Chromium's default font sizes through the DevTools protocol (`Page.setFontSizes`, what chrome://settings/fonts changes: 16 px and 13 px fixed-width times the zoom), not a `font-size` on `<html>`. The app's breakpoints are in rem (`sm` 40rem, `md` 48rem, `lg` 64rem), and a media query's rem is the browser's default size, which an `<html>` style does not move: with the style, a 768 px tablet at 200 % text kept the desktop header and squeezed a two-column grid to 14 px wide cells, a layout no reader ever gets; with the real setting it gets the phone layout, as the reader would. Run 3 used the style and reported 351 `textOverflow` and 599 `outOfViewport` findings; run 4 with the setting, 241 and 414.

**The languages.** `en`, `fi`, `sv`, `et`, and the pseudo-languages `qps-long` and `qps-short` (D95), each page loaded once per language and laid out at every viewport.

**Pseudo-localization (D95, `src/i18n/pseudo.ts`).** Two made-up languages generated from `en.json` the first time a page reads one (`messages['qps-long']` is a getter, so no bundle builds them otherwise), the way Google (`en-XA`), Microsoft (`qps-ploc`) and Mozilla test a layout's room for languages they do not ship yet:

- `qps-long`: every letter accented from Latin-1 and Latin Extended-A (`a` → `á`, `t` → `ť`: the `latin-ext` subset the fonts load, so a missing glyph shows at once), every vowel doubled (longer words, which cannot wrap, like Finnish compounds), padded with accented filler words (`ľöñğ`, `ťéšť`, `ĥéřé`, `ýéš`, `ŵöřď`) up to IBM's expansion for the string's length, and wrapped in `[ ]` so a clipped string shows a missing bracket. "Catalogue" (9 characters, target 18) is `[Çáaťáaľöoğüuée ľöñğ]`; "Monthly income" (14, target 26) is `[Möoñťĥľýy íiñçöomée ľöñğ ťéšť]`. Over the whole English file the long language is at least 1.3 times as long.
- `qps-short`: every word cut to its first letter and its consonants, at most four letters ("Catalogue" → `Ctlg`, "Send me a sign-in link" → `Snd m a sgn-in lnk`): under three quarters of the English, for layouts that assume a text fills its box.
- `{placeholders}` are kept exactly: the names, amounts, dates and ids put into them come from the data, which a translator does not touch either; numbers and dates format like English (`intlLocale`).

IBM Globalization Design's text-expansion guideline, the lower bound of each band (`expansionFactor`):

| English length (characters) | Expansion | Factor |
| --------------------------- | --------- | ------ |
| up to 10                    | 100–200 % | 2      |
| 11–20                       | 80–100 %  | 1.8    |
| 21–30                       | 60–80 %   | 1.6    |
| 31–50                       | 40–60 %   | 1.4    |
| 51–70                       | 31–40 %   | 1.31   |
| over 70                     | 30 %      | 1.3    |

The pseudo-languages are URL languages like `fi` (`/qps-long/catalogue`). `isLocale(value, env)` accepts them only while the demo tools are on (D74: development, the tests, a pitch build with `DEMO_TOOLS=1`; the switch is `src/demo-tools-switch.ts`, a module that imports nothing, so the language list the browser bundle carries imports no server module), so a plain production build answers 404; the language switcher lists only the four shipped languages. Texts held per shipped language outside the message files (the data pack's question texts on the apply and application pages) go through `translated(locale, read)`, which runs the English through the generator for a pseudo-language.

**Worked example, the audit chain's badge.** On `/fi/demo` at 320 px, the anchoring badge "Ankkuroitu tietokannan ulkopuolelle · merkintään N asti" is a `shrink-0` pill that cannot wrap: it spans 50 to 382 px, so the page is 382 px wide in a 320 px viewport (`outOfViewport` and `pageScroll`); on `/fi/audit` 33 to 365 px. On `/demo` at 320 px the English badge, "Anchored outside the database · up to entry N", spans 50 to 330 px, the Swedish one 50 to 349 and the Estonian one 50 to 351 (on `/audit`, which starts it 17 px further left, English fits and Swedish and Estonian reach 332 and 334); at 360 px only the Finnish one still spills (to 382 px). It was the one finding of the four shipped languages at the default text size apart from the life stamp's animation and one Finnish date (`4. marraskuuta 2029` on card 1's contract, 115 px in a 106 px box at 320 px). The fix (`ui/badge.tsx`): the pill keeps `shrink-0` and gains `max-w-full` and `wrap-anywhere`, so it is at most as wide as its row, 270 − 50 = 220 px on `/demo` at 320 px (the card's content box), and its label wraps onto a second line inside the pill (40 px tall instead of 24). Measured on the built app after the fix: on `/fi/demo` it spans 50 to 270 px at 320 px and 50 to 310 at 360 px, on `/fi/audit` 33 to 287 and 33 to 327, and the page is exactly as wide as the viewport (320, 360) in every case; the English badge wraps the same way.

## Decisions

- **D98** wide screens: wider lists, narrow forms, the contract at its width (decided by Johannes on 2026-11-10).
- **D99** the header's links fold into the menu below 1024 px, for everyone (decided by Johannes on 2026-11-10).
- **D95** pseudo-localization for the layout tests, gated by the demo tools switch (this page).
- **D74** the demo tools switch the pseudo-languages ride on.
- **D91** the browser tests on the built app this spec runs in (one worker, reset and reseed per test, the actor through the real routes).

## Pure functions

`src/i18n/pseudo.ts` (no clock, no I/O, Vitest in `pseudo.test.ts`):

- `expansionFactor(length: number): number`: the IBM lower bound for an English string of `length` characters (table above).
- `pseudoLocalize(message: string, mode: 'long' | 'short'): string`: one message in a pseudo-language; placeholders and blank messages are kept.
- `pseudoMessages(source, mode): Record<K, string>`: every message of a language, the keys unchanged.

`src/i18n/locales.ts`:

- `isLocale(value: string, env = process.env): value is Locale`: a shipped language always, a pseudo-language only while `demoToolsOn(env)`.
- `translated(locale: Locale, read: (shipped: ShippedLocale) => string): string`: `read(locale)` for a shipped language, the English pseudo-localized for a pseudo one.

Test code, in the page (`e2e/layout.ts`):

- `checkLayout(page, { phone }): Promise<{ problems, metrics }>`: the checks above at the current viewport; `problems` is `{ check, element, detail }[]` with the element as `tag.class "text"` and the numbers in `detail`.
- `checkTextScales(page): Promise<LayoutProblem[]>`: the `textNotScaling` check; puts the page back afterwards.
- `useViewport(page, viewport): Promise<void>`: the viewport size and the browser's text size, then `document.fonts.ready` and two animation frames.
- `VIEWPORTS`, `TEXT_ZOOMS` (`[1.5, 2]`), `LOCALES`.

## Open questions

- **A word longer than its line.** On a 390 px phone at 200 % text a long Finnish word in a heading or a narrow card still has to break (`Kirjautumislinkkisi` in a 326 px line), without a hyphen: `hyphens: auto` is off because it also breaks words that fit (the fixes above). The `brokenWord` check lets such a word break and lists every word that had room. Shortening the longest Finnish words, or hyphenating only words longer than their line (no CSS for it today), is open.
- **The header's menu from 768 to 1023 px.** A tablet now gets the menu, signed out too, where it had the links (D99). If the team wants the links back at tablet width, they need shorter labels or a second row by design.
- **An animation that ends before the check.** `animatedOverflow` samples the animations still in `document.getAnimations()`: `stamp-press` (fill `both`) stays there; `price-flip` and `audit-card-in` end without a fill and are missed if they finished before the check (the review's finding 7). Neither moves a box sideways today.
- **Chromium only.** `Page.setFontSizes` is a DevTools protocol call; Firefox and WebKit would need their own way to set the reader's text size.
- **The metric counts scrolled content.** The widest element is taken across `<main>`, a table inside its own scroller included: card 1's contract reports 1057 px in Finnish and 1263 px in `qps-long` against 1024 px in English.
